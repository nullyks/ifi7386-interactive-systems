# Activity 05 - Candidate Idea Screen

[← Session 1](../README.md) · [← Previous activity](04-problem-candidates-and-context-scan.md) · [Glossary](../../../resources/glossary.md) · [Academic readings](../../../resources/academic-readings.md)

**Time:** 14 minutes, including the instructor checkpoint · **Format:** team comparison → checkpoint

## Goal

Select a project direction through explicit discussion of user access, intended value, interaction potential, safety, ethics, feasibility, and team motivation.

## Scoring rule

Use **0** for weak or unknown, **1** for possible, and **2** for strong. Scores make uncertainty visible. They do not make the decision for you. A high total cannot make an unsafe or inaccessible project acceptable.

## Criteria

**Criteria** are the questions or standards you use to compare ideas.

1. **User access** - Can the team observe or speak with at least one relevant person before Session 2?
2. **Specific context** - Is there a recognisable place, time, activity, trigger, and constraint?
3. **Meaningful outcome** - Would improvement support the user's ability, understanding, comfort, access, coordination, or experience?
4. **Observable interaction** - Can the team connect input, system response or state, output, feedback, and user action?
5. **Course fit** - Is there a realistic and safe role for a physical or sensed input and a physical or digital output?
6. **Feasibility** - Can the team build and study a small but complete scenario during the course?
7. **Ethics and safety** - Can the project avoid unnecessary personal data, manipulation, surveillance, and risks the team cannot manage? It must not be relied on for health or safety.
8. **Team motivation and learning** - Does the direction interest several members and allow contributions and learning across the team?

## Steps

1. Score each candidate individually - 2 minutes.
2. Compare differences - 4 minutes. Discuss the criteria with the largest disagreement first.
3. Eliminate a candidate if user access, safety, or minimum feasibility cannot be resolved.
4. Select one working direction - 2 minutes. Record the decision and one parked alternative.
5. Prepare the checkpoint statement - 1 minute.
6. Use the remaining time for the checkpoint and any required revision.

### Checkpoint statement

> Our primary user is **[specific role]**. In **[specific context]**, the user needs to **[goal]**, but **[problem or breakdown]**. We can investigate this by **[practical way to collect evidence]**.

## Worked example

**Candidate:** shared study-room comfort cue

| Criterion | Score and reasoning |
|---|---|
| User access | **2** - the team can observe or speak with students who use study rooms |
| Specific context | **2** - place, group activity, and long-session trigger are clear |
| Meaningful outcome | **1** - value is plausible but has not been checked with users |
| Observable interaction | **2** - a sensed or simulated value can change a state and produce feedback the user can notice |
| Course fit | **2** - plausible sensor, physical indicator, and web status |
| Feasibility | **2** - if the first version uses simulated data and one main scenario |
| Ethics and safety | **1** - the cue must not be presented as a medical or safety guarantee |
| Team motivation and learning | **2** - opportunities for research, design, making, coding, and evaluation |

**Interpretation:** The idea may continue as a working hypothesis, but the team must investigate whether users want a background signal, what comfort means in practice, and how to stop the signal from appearing more reliable than it is.

## Possible checkpoint outcomes

- Proceed with the working hypothesis.
- Revise the user or context.
- Narrow the main scenario.
- Clarify access to evidence.
- Remove a safety or privacy risk.
- Park the direction and select another candidate.

## Output

A selected direction, a short decision rationale, a parked alternative, and approval or a specific revision request.
